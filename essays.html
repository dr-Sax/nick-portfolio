Light-Weight Counter Realities

December 2024

This research essay was written as final for Aras Ozgun's Mobile Media course at The New School. This research was on locative media through the method of infrastructural analysis. The essay covers historical developments of existing location infrastructures including GPS, Bluetooth, Wifi, and RFID. Serving as a ground, these infrastructures provide context from which to assess emerging forms of location techniques including Pedestrian Dead Reckoning (PDR) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). The localization company called Pointr is used as a case study for which a novel patent analysis method was devised to assess technological convergences. It is proposed that the indoor positioning systems are converging with infrastructures of autonomous vehicles and ride sharing based on cluster analysis of a 3D network graph of their patents. Secondly, an epistemological approach was taken to evaluate SLAM as a form of vision with emphasis on methods to take care in Stiegler's terms of this technology as it is integrated in human culture. A DIY method is taken to propose light weight mixed reality or 'counter-realities' which could be constructed by individuals through open-source means with a wide range of expression.

Voice and its Audio-Contents

December 2024

This research essay was written as a final for my media theory course at The New School instructed by Fabiola Hanna. Throughout the semester, I was interested in embodiment, datafication, objectivity, and voice and audio technologies. In the essay, I traced historical lineages of recording technologies used in psychotherapy of the 1930's and connected it with current day use of machine learning audio analysis softwares including Sonde Health's mental fitness app. Further, I discuss signal processing pioneer Dennis Gabor's wavelets and how it treats information (audio signals) as grains or windows of time-frequency content. I follow with a discussion of audio engineering artists including Curtis Roads and Iannis Xenakis to speculate upon alternative treatments of sound through interactive means such as the UPIC System. Lastly, I assess William Burrough's cut up method and draw in Katherine Hayles as well as Jonathan Sterne to assess disembodiment of voices and ways of reconnecting with lost voices through technology.

Leveling the Playing Field in the Social Sciences

April 2024

This research essay was inspired by my own past experiments with automated webscraping and crawling as well as conversations with collegues on algorithmic nature of multimedia web and app recommender interfaces. The research investigated existing techniques called agent-based tests in the field of computational journalism. I looked at experiments carried out by Nicholas Diakopoulos to evaluate social media's treatment of journalism from major news sources. These techniques were studied to better understand how to replicate the methods as well as build awareness for challenges and limitations of the method.
